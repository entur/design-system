# Route B: raster first, then strokes

Natural, figurative people and animals are hard to build anchor by anchor: placed by hand from coordinates, a figure comes out stiff and simplified — a gingerbread figure, not an Entur figure — and an animal comes out geometric, without the observed detail of the library animals. For people, animals and scenes with them, generate a PNG first with an image model that takes the library's own illustrations as style references, review it, then turn it into strokes.

Use route A (the script) for signs, landscapes, and objects or vehicles made of exact geometry; it is exact and needs no credits. Everyday objects and vehicles in side view may come this way too, as the Figma plugin draws them.

## 1. Gather references

- **Style references** (image inputs): two to four standard-mode PNGs from the library that are closest to the motif, from `apps/documentation/src/downloads/illustrations/<Category>/<Motif>/PNG/`. For people pick people of the same kind and pose: Woman holding package, Man checking the time, Man and woman hugging, Woman walking with luggage; for staff add `Entur staff/Woman/PNG/Woman staff.png` or the man. For animals use animal PNGs; for a bear, or any animal on snow or ice, add the designer-finished polar bear (step 13 below; in Figma the section Isbjorn in the Illustration Library, in the plugin the example Polar bear under Dyr): library scale, small round ears, the snow in Peach and no mouth; its plain and background versions show which shading each keeps. For objects: Ticket, Calendar, Clock, Notification, and also `Hands/Clapping`, `Hands/Thumbs up`, `Concepts/Data` and `Concepts/Digital Shared system`, the same object construction with Peach sparkle strokes, a Peach ring or Peach lines. For vehicles: the five in `Transport/`.
- **Newer people** (not in the downloads yet): the five newest sets in the People section of the Figma Illustration Library, made from the «People WIP» source files. They are Man standing with backpack, Woman conductor, Business traveller running (front and side view), Group trip with children, and Business traveller with suitcase. The Figma plugin keeps PNG exports of them as references. They carry more colour per figure than the older people: lavender clothes, Peach garments, several Peach items in the group, and coral staff panels on the conductor. Take line, proportion, clothing and pose from them, but keep the colour rules (rule 8): one Peach element, and coral only on Entur staff. Never mix in illustrations from outside the library.
- **Content reference:** the user's own photo, or a freely licensed one, may go in as an image input for pose and situation. A stock illustration or another illustrator's work (watermark, credit line) never goes in as an image input: the model copies what it is shown. Describe the scene in words instead and tell the user so in one line.

## 2. Generate

Use only tools Entur has approved: **Figma, Claude and Illustrator**. Figma's own image generation makes the draft and spends AI credits on the Figma plan; nothing else needs an account.

### Default: Figma image generation

- **In the Figma app** (a designer making the draft): Actions › Make an image, with two or three library PNGs attached through **+** as references and the prompt template below. References keep the line, the proportions and the colours closest to the library. Corrections go through Edit image on the draft (§3), which keeps what already works.
- **From Claude** (an agent making the draft): the Figma MCP tool `generate_image`, `model: "gemini-3.1-flash-image"`, `width` and `height` 2048, into a Figma file the user names or a new one (`create_new_file`). It takes a prompt only — no reference images, no draft to edit — so write the prompt as below under Fallback, and download the result from the `image.url` it returns. The gpt-image models it also offers are untested for this style.
- **Credits:** every run spends Figma AI credits on the file's plan, and each needs the user's approval, reruns included. So that this is one decision and not many, tell the user the whole plan before the first run — one generation plus up to two correction rounds — and then ask for each run as it comes.

### Fallback: prompt only

Without reference images (`generate_image`, or Make an image with nothing attached) the style drifts further; spell out every rule, including the line ("dark navy ink like a dip pen, swelling slightly in the middle and thinning at both ends") and the colours with their hex values. Drop the template's lines about attached references.

### Only where approved: Weave

Weave (app.weavy.ai, owned by Figma) runs Nano Banana 2.1 with reference images through the Figma MCP's `weave_*` tools, but it is a separate service with its own account, plan and credits, not part of the Figma licence. It is not approved at Entur: do not use it or offer it. Where an organisation has approved it, its runs ask for a cost approval each, like Figma's.

Ask for a square PNG, 2048 × 2048 where the model allows it. Use JPG only if the user asks for it: compression smears thin lines and makes tracing worse.

### Prompt template

Fill in the brackets; keep every rule, even the ones that seem obvious. A slot labelled [For …:] or [Optional for …:] belongs to the kinds it names (people, animals, objects, vehicles): for those, keep its text without the label; for the others, leave it out, and drop a bullet that is left empty. For an animal, the Peach element is never left out: name the prop or the marking (rule 19); for an object it is one small detail of it, never the whole object (rule 8). For coral take the first option — no coral or red — for animals, people, objects and scenes; the second only for a vehicle on its line, the third only for Entur staff (rule 8).

```text
Line illustration in exactly the style of the attached reference images (the Entur Linje illustration library).
Scene: [one sentence: who, doing what, with which one prop].

Style rules:
- Plain dark navy line (#181C56), even width, round line ends, a very slight hand-drawn tremor; some contours are left open.
- Flat fills only. No gradients, no shadows, no texture, no outlines thicker than the line.
- White (#FFFFFF) is the main fill. [A pale garment, such as trousers, a scarf or a hat band, is pale grey-lavender (#EAEAF1), not lavender.] Peach (#FFBF9E) for exactly one element: [which]; it is the only highlight colour. [No coral or red anywhere, not on a comb, a wattle, a tongue, cheeks or clothes | Coral (#FF5959) only for the line under the vehicle | Coral (#FF5959) only for the Entur staff shoulder and sleeve panels].
- Colour fills sit slightly offset from their line, so a thin sliver of white shows along one side.
- [For people: people are drawn naturally, like an observed drawing: realistic adult proportions about 7.5 heads tall, a small head, a relaxed pose with weight on one leg or a slight turn, loose clothes with only 2–5 tapered fold marks, hands with fingers, shoes with soles.]
- [For people: faces are white and have NO EYES and NO MOUTH: only a nose line. Glasses are allowed.]
- [Entur staff: dark navy uniform with coral shoulder and sleeve panels and a small white name badge.]
- [For people: furniture is simplified: a table has four legs or none, and no leg crosses a person.]
- [Animals: drawn naturally, like an observed sketch of the real animal — correct anatomy, but minimal: long smooth contours with a few gaps, no fur ticks along the outline, no cluster of fur strokes on the chest or neck, at most three or four short fur marks in all and only where they show the species, one inner line per ear; a bear's ears (polar bear included) are small and round, set close to the head, never a dog's flap. Every animal is about the same size in the frame, whatever its real size: a squirrel, a hen or a seagull as big as a goat or a bear, its body filling about two thirds of the frame on its longer side, never small and never touching the edges. The animal keeps a small dot eye and a small dark nose; no mouth line, no smile, no big cartoon eyes, no clothes. It has exactly one small peach (#FFBF9E) area, about 1 % of the image: a prop it holds, wears or has beside it (a collar, a ball, a ticket, a leaf), or one marking of its own (a chest patch, horns) as a flat shape with a rough edge and no outline. Peach on the body — a bird's beak or bill, comb, wattle and feet — is flat peach (#FFBF9E) with no dark outline around it; the head line stops where the beak begins but runs on under a comb; on a bird the beak and feet are its peach. The line swells slightly in the middle and thins at both ends, like a dip pen.]
- [For objects: one everyday thing on its own, slightly turned, no people or animals, no face; the line swells slightly like a dip pen; #EAEAF1 only on a side or back face.]
- [For vehicles: one vehicle in strict side view, long and low, facing right; white body; windows as solid navy fills with one or two short white glint strokes; no people visible; no disc; under it one coral (#FF5959) line: solid for rails, dashed for a road, a gentle wave for water.]
- Plain white background. [For animals and people: one simple thin navy ground line under the subject, in one or two long pieces, running a little past it: no row of dashes, no second line, nothing drawn below it (no snow mounds, stones or dashes under the line; a mound or rock sits on the line, behind the subject), no grass unless the scene asks for it — and then only one small tuft of two or three blades at the foot of something.] [For a snow scene: the snow is the one peach accent — one snowflake, a few dots and the low mound lines on the ground line, drawn as thin peach (#FFBF9E) lines and dots, no navy, no fill.] [For objects: no ground line: the object floats, alone or on the disc.] [Optional for animals: a soft pale grey-lavender disc (#EAEAF1) behind the upper body.] [Optional for objects: a soft pale grey-lavender disc (#EAEAF1) behind the object.]
- No text, no logos, no frame. Square, 1:1.

Use the references only for line, proportion and colour. Do not copy any figure or composition from them.
```

## 3. Review the PNG — before the user sees it

Review every draft yourself; never show a first draft as a result or wait for the user to point out what this review catches. Look at it next to the style references at the same size and run the Step 5 checklist in `SKILL.md`. Write down every deviation, fix them in an edit round, and review again. Up to two edit rounds; then trace. Models tend to slip on the same things:

- eyes or a mouth drawn anyway, or a cartoon face
- accessories drawn twice (two earcups, a split headband)
- big heads, blocky bodies, mitten hands — the corporate-illustration habit
- gradients, soft shadows, extra colours, a coloured background
- lines too thick or too even, like a vector icon
- text or logos
- the ground as a row of dashes, as two lines or heavier than the contours; grass tufts along it or on both sides when the scene does not need nature
- animals: geometric or blobby shapes, a cartoon face, big eyes or a smile, soft grey gradients in the fur, the animal small in the frame or crammed to its edges: every library animal's body spans 160–256 of 320 on its longer side, whatever its real size (rule 26)
- animals: more line than the library animal next to it — fur ticks along the back, a cluster of fur strokes on the chest or neck, several lines inside an ear (rule 18)
- animals: no Peach at all, or Peach spread over the body instead of one prop or marking (rule 19), or Peach as a patch on the ground, a snow mound or a stone
- anything drawn below the ground line — snow mounds, stones, dashes; a bear with a dog's big flap ears instead of small round ones
- peach on the body outlined in navy — a beak, a comb, a wattle, feet — or a beak in another colour: ask for "the beak, comb, wattle and feet are flat peach shapes with no outline; the head line stops at the beak and runs on under the comb" (rule 20)
- coral or red on an animal, a person or an object — a hen's comb and wattle, a tongue, cheeks, a garment: make it Peach or paper in an edit round (rule 8)
- a sign, prop or tail closer than 16 units to the canvas edge

Do not repair pixels, and do not start over. **In the Figma app**, select the draft, choose Edit image, attach the library reference that shows the fix, and ask for only the changes with the prompt below. **Through `generate_image`**, which has no image input, run the full prompt again with the same numbered changes appended as "Corrections from the last attempt", the pose and what worked described in words, and keep whichever draft is closer:

```text
Edit the first image. Keep everything else exactly as it is: [pose, hands, face, props, line, colours].
Make only these changes:
1. [UNIFORM: coral ONLY as a shoulder yoke and one narrow sleeve stripe, like the second image.]
2. [...]
The face keeps NO EYES and NO MOUTH, only the nose. No text. Square 1:1.
```

An edit keeps what already works; a new generation throws it away, which is why Edit image in the app is the better correction when a designer is at hand. One or two correction rounds are normal, and each costs the same as a generation. Near-white noise in the background (values around 249–255) is invisible and does not survive tracing; it needs no cleaning.

The approved PNG is a **draft**: fine for a sketch, a concept or a review, never as the final library asset.

## 4. Turn it into strokes

Final assets are live strokes in three colour modes.

**In Illustrator, automated** (macOS, Illustrator running; no plugin):

```bash
node scripts/illustrator-trace.mjs <draft.png> --out <dir> --name <Name>
```

For an animal or object add `--construction animals --carriers <profiles.ai> --backdrop --underlay` (details below), and for an object `--object` as well (below).

It drives Illustrator through AppleScript `do javascript` and writes `<Name>.ai` with live strokes, `<Name>.svg`, `<Name>-darkmode.svg` and `<Name>-contrast.svg`. An animal with a disc also gets the library's two versions, each with its dark and contrast files: `<Name>-background.svg` (the disc, no tint shading on the body) and `<Name>-plain.svg` (no disc, the shading kept); `<Name>.svg` keeps both, for the check against the draft. Options: `--app` for another Illustrator install (default Illustrator 2026), `--size` (artboard in pt, default 360, which puts a figure at library scale), `--threshold` (line darkness, default 110), `--max-stroke` (widest line traced as a stroke, default 20; at 14 the thicker pieces of a contour drop out), `--contour-share` (share of lines that get the contour weight, default 0.25), `--roughen` (add Roughen to the strokes; off by default), `--fill-rough` and `--ink-shift` (rough fill edges and offset ink clothing; off, and not in the reference drawings), `--no-features` (leave out the eyes and noses put back on animals, below), `--object` (an object: no eyes or noses, and the disc's pale colour only on its faces, below), `--vehicle` (a vehicle's body and window roles, below), `--no-simplify` (keep every traced line on an animal, below). A 2048 px draft can take several minutes, more with other large documents open. The same work, and the finishing steps below, are available to agents as MCP tools (`illustrator_status`, `illustrator_run_jsx`, `illustrator_trace_draft`, `illustrator_scale_head`, `illustrator_smooth_region`, `illustrator_add_details`):

```bash
claude mcp add -s local illustrator-bridge -- node <skill folder>/scripts/illustrator-mcp.mjs
```

What the script does, and why — the same steps apply when you trace by hand:

- **Line layer:** Black and White trace, **Strokes only**, method **Abutting**. With Fills on, the lines merge with the ink areas they touch (uniform, hair) into one filled region. With Overlapping, white areas are shapes stacked on a solid silhouette, and Ignore White deletes them, leaving a filled silhouette.
- **Fill layer:** Limited colours, Abutting, Fills. Every area snaps to the nearest palette colour. Enclosed white is kept as `paper`, because it turns light grey in dark mode; only the white background that touches the artboard edge is removed. Ink areas stay only when they are wide (mean width over ~4 pt: a uniform, hair, shoes); thin line-like ink is dropped, since the line layer draws it. On animals and people the ink comes from the draft's pixels instead (below, Ink masses), and on people the paper and colour do too (below, People).
- **Strokes:** ink colour, round caps and joins. The thickest quarter of the lines gets 1.5 pt, the rest 1.0 pt — by traced width, or by length (long outer contours) when the draft's lines are all equally thick. Library people land at about that mix; an all-1.5 figure looks heavy and coarse next to them.
- **Fills smooth, lightly shifted.** A low path fidelity keeps colour areas to a few anchors instead of a jagged edge, and no Roughen goes on fills. Colour areas (not ink) are shifted 0.6 pt right and 0.3 pt down, the size of the offsets in hand-made reference people.
- **Roughen on an animal's strokes.** An animal's strokes get Roughen 0.2 % (relative, detail 63), the same as its ground line, as the designer roughened the traced polar bear's outline; `--no-roughen` turns it off. People and objects get none by default: their draft already carries the hand tremor, and on large fills a relative Roughen makes edges hairy. `--roughen` turns it on for them.
- **Modes by palette mapping.** Every white becomes `paper`; recolour white clothing to `cloth` by hand in the dark file if the motif has it. With `--vehicle` the script gives the vehicle its roles itself (below). The backdrop circle shares its standard colour with `tint`, but the script gives the Circle layer the `backdrop` values from `palette.md` in the dark and contrast files: Ebony 20 at 30 % opacity and Blue 90, where small tint fills turn Ebony 30 and Blue 40.

With `--construction animals`, for animals and objects:

- **Artboard and weights:** 320 pt, contour 2.1 and detail 1.7 at the widest point.
- **Width profiles** (`--carriers`): every traced line gets a live width profile, copied from the strokes named `swell` and `point` in the carriers file (`handoff.md`); lines shorter than 12 pt, such as fur marks, get `point`.
- **Backdrop** (`--backdrop`): the traced disc is lumpy and often traces as a white interior inside a grey rim. It is replaced by the library's 4-anchor circle with Roughen 0.65 % on a Circle layer, and the traced white interior and rim fragments are removed. White marks the draft drew on the disc (snowflakes, stars, dots) come back as Peach shapes on a Marks layer above the circle, as the designer coloured the polar bear's snow, so they stay in the plain version too: white pieces well inside the disc (within 90 % of its radius, clear of a brushed rim's streaks) that touch no line, a small one only when it is a round dot. Under the polar bear that kept three snowflakes and five dots. The circle is measured in the draft's pixels — the largest connected area of the disc's pale grey-lavender, its radius from the longer side, measured from the top — so it still comes in when the subject covers a third of the disc or more (Hens family), and a grey wing beside it does not stretch it. Only a plausible disc counts — at least 30 % of the width, round, at least a third filled, near the middle — so a draft without one (two seagulls in flight, with pale wings) gets no circle: the library has animals with and without the disc.
- **Underlay** (`--underlay`): paper under the animal, traced from its silhouette in the draft. Without it, white areas the colour trace cannot tell from the background — a tail that reaches past the disc — stay unfilled, and the ground line shows through. Every figure gets its own shape (a chick standing apart from the hen too), holes in the silhouette stay open, the paper stops at the top of the ground line except inside a closed figure that crosses it (an egg sitting in the grass), a body the circle cuts is continued past it (below), and pockets closed off between grass blades and the line are left out; each would show as a pale patch in dark mode. The paper comes in a second pass, after the lines exist: the draft's silhouette is cut back to the finished lines (gaps up to 2 % of the width closed), so its edge sits under the middle of every line — not past a line end that thins, and not past a fur mark the simplify step removed, which left a white rim in dark mode. White and grey slivers the colour trace keeps in the draft's thin gaps between a line and its fill (mean width under 1.5 pt; grey only under 20 pt²) go, and so does every white piece lying mostly outside the figures, judged by points over its area (a white triangle between a disc's edge, a sign and its post) — outside both the draft's silhouette and the closed finished lines, which leaves an open cloud out even where it closes against a wing in the draft. Those showed as a white double line along the seagulls' cloud and white strips outside their contours in dark mode. Large pieces of the traced disc cut off by a line (under the doves' wire) go with the backdrop step: a lighter patch in dark mode. Inside the disc white counts as figure, but outside it a fluffy outline's gaps let the background in, so the draft's paper can stop exactly at the circle's edge (a white Samoyed with no paper below the circle). Where the paper is cut along a good stretch of the rim (5 % of the width or more), it is continued into what the finished lines enclose (gaps up to 1.6 % of the width closed), through the gaps of the outline but never across a drawn line, down to the ground line; a white pocket that only touches the rim for a few pixels (between a hen and an egg) stays open. Then the paper's edge is fitted to the strokes (rule 21): pass one writes the strokes' geometry, and walking round each piece of paper every contour stroke hands over to the next with a straight chord — from its free end on in its own direction to the next stroke, else from end to end — and paper past the chords goes while a dent short of them fills. Nothing changes deeper than 1.5 % of the width, no chord runs to the ground line (the gap between two feet stays background), and the ground band keeps its footprint. On the Samoyed this took the visible difference from a designer's hand-cut paper down by about 40 %. The final mask is smoothed (a 4 px blur at 1024, halved), so a ragged edge does not trace as stairs. An animal's paper is traced smoother than an object's (path fidelity 12 against 20), as the designer cut the polar bear's from 44 points to 33. The paper is the figure's one white surface: the colour trace's own white over it (on the polar bear a copy of the whole silhouette with the ear, the eye and a sliver) goes, as on an object. A line with figure on both sides of it for most of its length (an inner leg line) is detail weight however long it is; contour weight is for lines with background beside them.
- **Ink masses:** dark wing and tail tips, a nose or a dark patch, with white streaks between the feathers. The mean-width rule drops those (four of six seagull tips went), so on animals and people the ink is opened in the draft instead — eroded by about 4 px at 1024, grown back and cut with the ink again, so the gaps stay white — and every piece of at least 400 px at 1024 is traced as an ink fill on top of the Fill layer, in a group named Ink. Lines are too thin to survive the opening; pieces lying wholly in the ground band (a thick dash) are left to the ground step. In Figma they bind to the same line variable as the outline, as on the library doves.
- **Vehicles** (`--vehicle`, people construction): the dark and contrast files give the library's vehicle roles. White areas become the `vehicle` body (Ebony 40 in dark mode, white in contrast). The windows' ink fills become `window` (Canary 30 in dark mode, Blue 70 in contrast), and the paper glints inside them stay paper. A window is a solid dark area of the draft once its lines are opened away: at least 1.5 % of the width across, filling most of its box, and centred in the upper three quarters of the vehicle. Wheels and small dark details sit lower or are smaller. On the library's bus, train, tram, boat and scooter this finds exactly the fills their dark files light up (5, 4, 4, 8 and none). The colour trace loses the body: its white joins the background through the open contour. So `--vehicle` brings the paper underlay as the body, with gaps up to 2 % of the width closed, walls at the left and right edge of the ink, and gaps along a row of up to 6 % of the width bridged: a bus's skirt line stops short of each wheel. A train's last car, which runs off with its lines and no end line, stays part of the body, as in the library's train. The window fills stay in the lines the underlay is cut back to, so a windscreen closes the nose.
- **People** (the default construction, not with `--vehicle` or `--underlay`; rule 25): found on a customer-service figure at a desk, where the colour trace gave one white compound under the person, the desk and every object, an ink band under the strokes that met the hair, notched hair with odd bits over the shoulder, and thin blue pieces along the hands and cuffs. Now:
  - _Ink masses_ come from the opened ink, as on animals, so the colour trace's ink, which keeps every line that touches the hair as part of it, goes. A person keeps masses from 150 px at 1024 (a suitcase wheel's solid core) whose core after the opening is at least (0.6 % of the width)²: a desk leg drawn 9 px thick survives the opening with a core one pixel across and came back as a dark block at its top. The mask is then made solid. A closing of 0.2 % of the width fills the nicks. A light hole inside a mass that is thin (mean width under 0.8 % of the width) or small is filled (a strand of shading). A highlight strip (luminance 110–200, as down a chair's edge) is taken in up to 0.8 % of the width, and the mass reaches 2 px further into anything darker than paper, so it meets the outline beside it under that outline's stroke. Near-white (luminance 235 or more) is never filled: the gap between a headset band and the hair, or the white middle of an earpiece, is drawn on purpose, and a white gap of up to 4 px between two masses is widened by 1 px on each side so Image Trace does not close it.
  - _Parts:_ a part is a pale area of the draft (luminance at least 200, nearly grey, so the light shading on a hand counts as the hand) with any colour area in it, closed by its lines, its seeds kept 1 px clear of the lines so a hairline gap does not join two parts. Every part and the outside grow into the lines around them at once, up to 0.8 % of the width, so neighbours meet in the middle of a line. Two large areas that meet through a channel of 3 px or less, where a line stops just short of the next, are split into two parts at the channel (cores 2 px clear of the lines; the channel goes to the nearer core): the line under the sleeve stopped 3 px short of the book, and the desk top ran on under the shirt. 3 px clear also cut a face from its neck where a headset boom stops short of the jaw, through open skin. A pocket under (2.4 % of the width)² — between two fingers, inside a lock of hair — joins the neighbour it shares most line with, one of its own tone first (a pale-grey corner of a suitcase joins the case, not the white beside it), so a hand is one shape. A part whose own pale pixels lie under an ink mass goes; a white strand drawn inside the hair, too thin for a seed, becomes a part of its own. The space under a desk or bench is a wide, flat area on the ground line (at least 20 % of the width, and twice as wide as tall) and stays open. Nothing in the pixels tells air from cloth or skin: the triangle between the torso, the upper arm and the forearm on the desk is as pale as the shirt and as triangular as the V-neck, so it joined the sleeve as a pocket. A point given with `--open` (artboard units, as in the SVG) makes the part under it background before the pockets join. Points given with `--clothes` join the parts under them, after the pockets, into one part named Clothes, outlined round all of them so it is one fill under its seams; the Figma plugin binds a node named Clothes to `Fill/Cloth`. Without `--clothes` the trace guesses (`--clothes ""` means no clothes). The face is the topmost white part at least (4 % of the width)² that the ink masses cover for 5 % or more, the hair running into it. The figure is every part reached from the face across a shared line, but not through the desk top (the part over the open space under the desk, or one wider than 60 % of the image), a tint part under (8 % of the width)² (a book; a bigger one is a grey jacket or a lavender sweater), a part mostly peach or coral (a screen), or a part that fills 93 % or more of its convex hull. That last test keeps objects out: the laptop, the suitcase and the monitor fill 94–99 %, the shirt and the skirt at most 91 %. A part under 40 % of the face that does not touch the face ends the way, so a hand does not bring in what it holds. In the figure, a part above the face's middle that touches it (a forehead over glasses, a headset band) is skin. A small part away from the face that touches the background, an ink mass or something outside the figure is a hand. The rest is clothes. On the customer-service figure the guess got every part but the hand at the chin, which touches the face like a collar piece. On the woman with the suitcase it got the face, the hands, the jacket and the skirt, and took the legs, a folder and the closed air beside the arm for clothes. `<Name>-parts.json` holds the parts before the clothes join: `{ viewBox, parts: [{ id, d, point, role, guessed }] }`. `d` is the part's outline in the SVG's units, `role` is `clothes`, `skin`, `object`, `tint` or `open`, and `guessed` says whether clothes-or-not came from the guess. `point` is the part's deepest pixel, the one furthest from its edge, so it lands in the same part on the next trace and goes back as `--clothes` or `--open`. The pockets that joined a part, at least (1.2 % of the width)², are listed after the parts with their part's role, so air inside the clothes can be marked. So are the parts left open, so they can be filled again. A part is Tint when most of its pale pixels are #EAEAF1-ish rather than white (the book), else white. Each part is drawn from its outline, followed along the pixel edges, thinned to the corners more than 1 px from a straight run and rounded into smooth points, in a group named Parts under the other fills. Image Trace joined parts that meet under a line, even 2 px apart, and tracing a mask per part failed now and then (MRAP). The guess also handles a desk. The desk top is the widest flat part (half the image wide, a tenth as tall), and tall narrow parts under it that reach the ground are its legs and join it: the designer drew the desk and its legs as one fill of 15 points. The legs under a desk meet the body only through the desk top, so a part under it joins the figure when the figure sits on top over most of the columns where it meets the desk's underside (the trousers under the torso, not the air beside them). Under the desk a sock leads on to its shoe, and a small part is not a hand. A white part below the desk top and within its width is air when the figure does not reach it and it reaches the ground, or is big and straight-edged (the gap between the chair's column and a leg). A big one also stops the way out from the face, so a white trouser leg next to it is reached from the thigh, not through the air. The trace guesses air only without `--open`: `--open ""` means none. A part counts as an object only when it fills 93 % of its hull and 60 % of its outline lies in straight runs longer than 4 % of the width: the round back of a shirt fills its hull as well as a monitor does (0.94), but only 55 % of it is straight, against the monitor's 82 % and the suitcase's 66 %. Garments join by tone: the dressed parts that share a line and are all white or all Tint become one Clothes each (the shirt, the trousers, the shoes). A part mostly lavender or blue-grey in the draft (trousers, a screen) is Tint, its lavender is no colour piece of its own, and a lavender piece left over is drawn Tint; lavender on an ink mass (a sheen on the chair) goes. A stroke that lies at least 80 % on an ink mass, widened by 0.6 % of the width, goes too: the designer drew the chair, the hair, the keyboard and the mouse without outlines. People get one ground line, as animals do: the flat traced pieces in the ground band go, and one Roughen line on a Ground layer under the fills takes their place. Each part's outline keeps the corners more than 0.25 % of the width off a straight run (2.6 px at 1024), about half the points of a 1 px tolerance. On the seated customer-service figure that gives 11 fills (8 white, 3 Tint) with about 250 points: the desk with its legs is one, the clothes are four garments (the shirt, the trousers in Tint, each shoe with its sock), and the monitor is Tint. A turn sharper than 60° is a corner point, and a smooth point's handles reach at most a third of their own segment: with fewer points, Catmull-Rom handles looped at the desk legs' joints and left a teardrop at the monitor's corner. The designer's hand trace has 23 fills and about 614 points in all, with the air under the desk open as here; the trace had 40 fills and 1348 points. Still by hand: a book against the body comes out as clothes, an object's line does not stop behind the body, and a desk-top line the draft breaks where the chair crosses it stays broken. On the figure at the desk: 11 parts instead of one white, and the air by the elbow open. They are Clothes (the two shirt halves, three collar pieces and the badge), the face and neck with the V-neck, each hand, the cup, the desk top, the monitor and its stand, and in Tint the book's top and side and a light strand in the hair.
  - _Colour_ stays only where the draft has a colour area of the piece's own role: lavender, #9EA0BD and Tint are opened by 0.4 % of the width, so the mid tones along a line's edge go (14 thin blue pieces on the hands, cuffs and book); peach and coral are kept thin too, since navy and white never mix to them (a headset band).
  - _Known limits:_ a white highlight strand that runs out to the hair's edge counts as outside, so it shows the background in dark mode, as a thin dark line in the hair; the space under a desk that the chair and legs split into tall pieces stays filled, as before.
- **The whole figure** (rule 22): the paper reaches under every part of a figure. Trimming the paper to the middle of a line never cuts through a thin stretch of the line into the paper on its other side; that left a dark crack along the inside of a snowman's outline in dark mode. If Illustrator fails a step with its MRAP error, or reports an object it has just made as invalid, the pass closes its own document and runs again after a short pause, up to three times. Both come and go with the same script; an Illustrator that has been open for days with large documents fails more often, and a restart clears it.
- **Objects** (`--object`): an object gets no eyes or noses, and inside the circle the disc's pale grey-lavender stays only on a face of the object: a pale area closed in by lines that lies on the object (paper makes up 40 % or more of the ring just beyond its line), such as a card's chip. Everything else pale there is the disc showing through, and the colour trace kept it as a tint fill: an opaque grey in dark mode and a light rim in contrast mode. A strip between a line and its paper, where the draft's paper sits a little off the line, goes, and the paper under it shows. The inside of an empty ring on the disc gets neither tint nor paper, so the circle shows there in every mode, as in the library. Found on the rebate ticket: a grey dot in an empty ring in dark mode and light rims at the notches in contrast mode. An empty ring counts only when its inside is a solid pocket, at least 0.8 % of the width thick on average. The thin pale band where a colour meets a white highlight (a scarf) is no ring. An object's figure is also kept whole, as the snowman needed:

  - Inside the circle only the disc's own pale is background. White and every colour on the disc (a scarf, a hat band, a nose) are figure with paper under them, in the draft's silhouette and when the paper is trimmed to the finished lines. Where an outline is open wider than the closing, under a hat brim or at a nose, the flood would otherwise take the whole head.
  - The shading (below) closes the figure where its outline is open, such as a ball whose bottom lies past the disc.
  - A small pocket closed in by the paper and lines gets paper, such as a thin white wedge under a hat brim. Empty rings are cut after this.
  - Short marks are not thinned as fur, since an object has none, so a scarf keeps its fringe. Specks up to 4 pt go, where an animal keeps those over 3 pt.
  - The colour trace's white lying on the paper goes in pass two, so a figure keeps one white surface, its paper. Before this, the white sat on top with holes where the buttons are, a little off its lines, and the background showed through in thin cracks. A glint in a window stays.

  Shading is the exception. A pale band inside a figure, between its outline and its white, like the crescent on a snowman's balls, is kept as a tint shape named Shade (Fill/BodyShadow in Figma), with the figure's paper under it. It has the disc's colour, and where the outline is open the band runs into the disc, so the colour trace merged it with the disc and the paper stopped short: the balls were cut off in dark mode. A pale pixel is in such a band when, along one of eight directions, it reaches a large white area through pale only and the opposite way a line, within 5 % of the width in all. A band counts when it is large and mostly at least 1.2 % of the width across, wider than the ticket's strips. Past the end of the line (the shadow wrapping round the bottom of the ball) the band may end at the page's white instead, but only to extend shading already found against a line. None of the library's objects gets any.

- **Eyes and noses** (on by default): a small dark eye or nose is too thin for the colour trace and too round for the line trace, so it drops out of both. The script finds them in the draft — dots that stand free, and thick spots left once the lines are eroded away, such as a nose at the end of the snout line — and puts each back as an ink ellipse on a Features layer on top. People never get them: an Entur person has no eyes (rule 13), and glasses are lines the line trace keeps. Spots in the band just above the ground line are never taken: there they are grass clumps, not eyes (Hens family had nine). A smaller animal in a group has a smaller eye, so a dot down to about 21 px in a 1024 px draft still counts when it is round, solid and stands free of other ink (the second seagull's eye).
- **Simplify** (on by default; `--no-simplify` turns it off): the draft usually has more line than a library animal (rule 18). Before the weights go on, the script drops specks under 3 pt, joins a contour broken into pieces end to end (within 4 pt, running on within 45°), replaces the ground with one plain line, and thins short fur marks (under 20 pt): a mark lying along a contour goes, and of marks within 20 pt of each other only the longest stays. Marks within 20 pt of an eye or nose (an inner ear line), toes and grass stay, but a short mark just under the nose is a mouth and goes (the designer took the polar bear's out); toes are measured from the ground line, not from the lowest line, since snow mounds or stones drawn below it would otherwise take the toes for fur. Toes come from the draft's pixels: a short upright dark tick rising off the line (narrow, at most 1.3 % of the width tall, free at the top, the figure beside it) is a toe. The traced strokes there go, and each toe is drawn as a thin stroke (1.1 pt, no width profile) of three points from foot to tip, as the designer drew the polar bear's six. The trace alone kept at best five: it joins a toe to the line or drops it as a speck. On the bear cub it took the trace from 80 to about 50 strokes. The ground: where the draft has a ground line (one dark run across a quarter of the width, found in its pixels), the traced dashes and short grass marks in that band go, and one plain line takes their place — ink, detail weight, Roughen 0.2 %, from the first to the last piece of the drawn line — on a Ground layer above the fills, so it shows across the paws as the designer placed it on the polar bear. It is drawn only where the draft draws it: a gap wider than 3 % of the width (a tail lying across it) breaks it. A drawn line tilts a little, so the script follows it out from the band at each end while it stays flat, and stops where it turns (a snow mound starting at its end): under the polar bear the band held only 66–186 of a line that runs 26–200. Only marks along the line go. What stands free below it goes, lines and colour (snow mounds, stones, a dash under it: the designer took them all out from under the polar bear): ink that touches the line nowhere once the line's own pixels are out, with no figure paper in its box, and at least 1.2 % of the height below the line where the line runs above it. A tail lying across the line (the fox's) or a contour dipping 2 pt under it (the Samoyed's haunch) stays. A short mark that touches a leg is a foot and stays, unless it lies flat on the line, which shows there anyway. Without a ground line in the draft, the dashes are joined into pieces at detail weight instead.

**Check the trace against the draft.** The trace must reproduce the draft — nothing lost, nothing added — except what the skill changes on purpose. After every trace, and before the finishing steps:

```bash
node scripts/trace-check.mjs <draft.png> <Name>.svg [--out <dir>] [--json] [--object] [--vehicle]
```

It renders the standard SVG at the draft's size (macOS `qlmanage` and `sips`, no dependencies, under a second at 1024 px) and compares pixel classes: ink by luminance, peach, coral, lavender, tint and paper by the nearest palette colour. It reports, in artboard units, ink missing from the trace (typed dot, mass or line: a lost eye, a dark wing tip, a line), ink added, colour areas lost or added, white fill outside the figures (a crescent or strip that only shows in dark mode), paper along the outside of a line (a white rim or double line, also only in dark mode), and paper missing inside a figure (the dark surface inside an outline — white in the draft, closed in both the draft and the trace; an opening between legs that closes against the ground line is left out). It also reports paper missing in a gap inside a figure (rule 22): rendered at twice the size on magenta, with the circle magenta too, any thin pocket where the disc or the page shows through inside a figure, such as a crack between the white and a line or a strip along a colour that sits off its line. An empty ring's inside is wider, and the space between two legs ends at the ground line, so neither counts. On the first snowman it found 19 such cracks that the other findings missed. A piece cut from the edge of a larger mark is ignored unless it is large, so contour rounding is not noise. It writes `trace-check.json` and `trace-check.png`, the draft faded with every finding boxed and numbered: red missing, blue extra, magenta paper outside.

An animal whose paper spans less than 160 or more than 256 of 320 on its longer side is reported as too small or too large (rule 26): scale it in the drawing, or ask the image model for a larger animal. Read every finding. **Intended:** fur and feather marks the simplify step thinned, drawing that stood free below the ground line (trace-check leaves it out of its findings), grass and dashes the plain ground line replaced, tint differences at the rim of the redrawn library circle, a red part turned Peach. For an object traced with `--object`, pass `--object` to the check too: pale strips that became paper on purpose are then not reported. For a vehicle pass `--vehicle`: its open ends are closed as in the trace, so a train's last car is not reported as paper outside the figure. **A fault:** anything else — fix it in the trace or by hand, and say in the result what is left. Two faults it found and the trace now handles: a transparent draft traced as one dark square (drafts with an alpha channel are flattened on white first), and birds on a wire whose wire was taken for ground (a long line with tails and feet below it is a perch and stays as drawn).

When `illustrator-trace.mjs` changes, run the trace and the check on a regression set before committing: the drafts behind Hens family, the two seagulls, the bear cub, the fox, the Samoyed and the polar bear (toes, ground line, what stands below it), and the library's `Animals/Doves/PNG/Doves-background.png`. Known gaps: the doves' tiny eyes are still missed, and a part whose outline has a gap wider than about 10 px at 1024 in the draft (the left seagull's far wing) gets no paper — trace-check reports it as paper missing inside a figure; add the paper by hand.

**In Illustrator, by hand:**

1. Place the PNG on a 360 pt artboard and lock it on a layer at 50 % opacity.
2. Line layer: Object > Image Trace, Mode Black and White, threshold so only the navy line survives, method Abutting. Under Advanced, create **Strokes** only, with a stroke width limit around 20 px for a 2048 px draft. Expand.
3. Select all lines. Stroke panel: 1.0 pt, Cap Round, Corner Round, Profile Uniform; then select the long outer contours and set them to 1.5 pt (about a quarter of the lines). Join broken pieces, delete specks; aim for few anchors.
4. Fill layer: trace the PNG again with Mode Limited colours, method Abutting, a low path fidelity and the palette as the swatch set, create Fills, delete the background white, nudge one to three colour areas 0.6 pt right and 0.3 pt down, and put the layer under the lines.
5. Build the dark and contrast versions with Recolor Artwork and the mapping in `palette.md`. Add the Roughen graphic style from `handoff.md` only if the lines look too clean.

**Finish** — the trace gives the construction; these steps give it the reference drawings' simplicity, proportions and life. Points are Illustrator points, y up; read them off a grid render of the traced file.

1. **Simplify accessories:** a traced headset, pair of glasses or strap often comes out doubled — a cushion plus a ring, a band split in two by a line. Delete the extra parts and redraw each as one shape (one band stroke, one earcup). Where a removed part cut through a silhouette, the silhouette keeps a slot; close it by removing those anchors (`illustrator_run_jsx`).
2. **Head size:** compare with a library person at the same height. If the head is bigger, run `illustrator_scale_head` with the neck point, the scale (0.8–0.9) and the head column. It scales head parts as wholes and moves the head anchors of any silhouette that runs into the body with a soft blend, so hair inside a hair-and-uniform shape shrinks too. Scaling parts one by one misses that hair, and the head keeps looking big.
3. **Smooth the hair:** the trace gives people solid hair from the draft's pixels (People, above); if its edge still looks lumpy, `illustrator_smooth_region` over the hair (default: every fourth anchor kept, three passes) gives one smooth edge. It also flattens small bumps inside the region, such as a microphone tip; redraw those afterwards.
4. **Fold and sheen marks:** add 2–5 tapered marks with the MCP tool `illustrator_add_details` (points in Illustrator points, y up; read them off a grid render of the traced file), or by hand with a width profile or a small filled shape. Paper-white on ink or colour, ink on light fills.
5. **Simplify props:** remove a lone table leg, or any leg that crosses a person; four legs or none.
6. **Animals — eyes and noses:** the trace puts them back on the Features layer. Check that each is there and the right size; draw one it missed as a small ink ellipse, and delete one that is not a feature (a dark button or hoof the colour trace already kept).
7. **Ground:** one simple line at detail weight, in one to three pieces (`style.md` §4), behind whatever lies across it. On animals the trace replaces it with one plain line on a Ground layer above the fills, drawn only where the draft draws it, so it shows under the paws and between the legs and breaks where a tail lies across it. The paper stops at the line, never below it, so no pale strip shows under the feet. Nothing stays below the line. On people move the ground line to a layer under the fills by hand where it crosses the figure (a coat hem, a chair base), so their fills hide it — the trace leaves it on top, because the colour trace's thin white strips around the dashes would cover it there; join the dashes and set the weight by hand. Grass is not removed automatically (a toe and a blade look alike to the trace): keep at most one tuft of two or three blades, where the scene gives it a reason (a pole, a bench leg), and delete the rest.
8. **Animals — less line:** look at the result next to a library animal at the same size. Delete what the simplify step left that the library would not draw: a fur tick on the back, a second mark on the chest, a staircase of short pieces along a contour (join those into one stroke). One fold line at the neck or shoulder can replace a run of fur.
9. **Animals — Peach:** the trace keeps a Peach area from the draft and shifts it off its line. If the draft brought none, add one now on the Fill layer, about 1 % of the canvas: a prop gets its own ink outline with the Peach shifted 0.6 pt off it; a marking on the body (a chest patch) has no outline and Roughen 1.5 %, relative, detail 51.
10. **Animals — peach on the body:** if the trace still has ink lines around a beak, comb, wattle, feet or another peach body part, delete the strokes that belong to the part — every anchor along its outer edge, away from the body, such as a comb's scalloped top. Keep the head's or body's own contour where it runs along the part's base (the forehead line under a comb), even when all its anchors lie within 1.6 pt of the peach: closeness alone also catches that line. A stroke that runs on toward another feature (the beak) is the head's, not the part's. Cut each contour that only passes the peach so it ends about 2 pt from it — at the boundary anchor, splitting the curve, not by dropping anchors; at a beak the head line stops with that gap. Then grow the peach shape by about 0.9 pt (Offset Path, expanded) so it covers where the line ran, a beak's tip a little past the old tip (rule 20). Horns and props keep their lines. Copying a path into another open document with `duplicate()` fails (MRAP): copy its anchors and handles instead.
11. **Animals — the paper's edge (rule 21):** the trace fits the paper to the strokes (Underlay above); check it in the dark-mode file. Two places it leaves for you: paper behind a fur tuft that curls outward off the contour, where a hand-cut edge runs straight past it from the stroke before to the stroke after, and paper between two feet on the ground line, where the line should show. Edit the Underlay shape there (Direct Selection, or Shape Builder against the strokes). trace-check reports paper along the outside of a line; a dent between strokes it does not report.
12. **Objects and figures — the designer's finish.** A designer's own finish of a traced snowman beside a tree (November 2026) is the reference for what follows the trace. Measured against the trace it came from:

    - **One paper shape per figure, nothing white on top.** The trace left one underlay under snowman and tree together (25,462 pt²) and three white pieces from the colour trace on top (tree 7,766, body 6,797 and head 2,593 pt²). The finish has two white shapes, one under the snowman (18,201 pt², 55 anchors) and one under the tree (8,741 pt², 41 anchors). The trace now drops an object's colour-trace white lying on the paper. Split an underlay that two figures share by hand, along the front figure's outline.
    - **A group per figure.** The snowman's lines, its ink fills (hat, arms, buttons), colours, shading and paper are one group, paper at the bottom, so the figure moves and scales as one. The designer scaled it up about 6 % from its feet. The tree's lines are a group of their own.
    - **Weight by depth** (rule 23): every snowman line is 2.5 pt and the tree's lines 2.0 and 1.5 pt, with the width profiles kept.
    - **Specks go:** three marks of 3.1–3.7 pt (beside the buttons, by the tree). The trace now drops an object's specks up to 4 pt.
    - **Smooth shading:** the shading crescents keep their shape with fewer anchors (18 to 7, 44 to 16).
    - **The ground line** (rule 9): one straight ink line under snowman and tree (2.5 pt, from the snowman's left to past the tree). The tree's trunk lines run down to it, and the trunk between them is an ink fill with one thin 1.1 pt line inside. The disc moves up 23 pt, so the line runs just below its edge (rule 10).
    - **A Peach accent with sparkles** (rule 24): three short 2 pt Peach strokes around the star.
    - **A glint** (rule 24): one short tapering white stroke of about 1.1 pt on the carrot nose, which stays Peach with no outline.
    - **Pale clothing** (rule 8): the scarf and hat band are pale grey-lavender (`#EAEAF1`), not lavender, so in Figma they bind to the Animal group's `Fill/BodyShadow`.

    Two things to do differently from that file. The ground line should be ink (`#181C56`), not a near-navy (`#212768`): only palette colours change in the dark and contrast files, so that line stays dark and vanishes on the dark surface. The ground line, the sparkles and the trunk also belong on the Outline and Fill layers, not the Circle layer, because the Figma binding gives anything in the Circle layer the disc's variable (`handoff.md`).

13. **Animals — the designer's finish: the polar bear.** A designer's own finish of a traced polar bear cub on snow (November 2026, two rounds) is the reference for an animal after the trace. The trace already does what they did to its construction: one white body fill, nothing standing free below the ground line, the ground line above the fills, six toes as thin three-point strokes at 1.1 pt, an inner leg line at detail weight, Roughen 0.2 % on the outline, no mouth line, the marks on the disc in Peach, and the two library versions (`-background` with the disc and no body shading, `-plain` without the disc and with it). What is left is drawing, measured against the trace of the same draft:

    - **Library scale** (rule 26). The bear is scaled up to fill the frame as the library's animals do: its body from 146 to 184 on its longer side (the library's run 160–256), the ground line under it 28–288 and the mounds beside it reaching 15–290.
    - **A small round ear.** The draft gave the cub a dog's flap; the finish redraws it about a third smaller, round and close to the head, with a thin inner line (1.5 pt). Its inner tint stays only in the plain version.
    - **The snow is the Peach.** No Peach patch on a mound: the snowflake (one, of short strokes, beside the head and breaking out of the disc), five dots and the two mound lines are Peach (`#FFBF9E`), the mounds as thin outlines only, no ink and no fill.
    - **Ground details behind the animal, on the line.** The two low mounds sit on the ground line, one on each side, behind the bear. Nothing is drawn below the line.
    - **Shading only where it says depth** (plain version): the far hind leg, the back edge of the front leg, the chest under the chin and the inner ear.
    - **The back in two lines.** The long back contour becomes two, the back and the haunch, with the paper fitted under them.
    - **Fur marks where they say something:** the two short marks move to the chest and the neck.

**In code** (no Illustrator): `node scripts/grid.mjs <draft.png> --format square`, render the grid, and redraw the centre lines with the script (route A), 12–25 anchors per contour of a person. Slower, and it loses some of the PNG's life, but it stays in the repository and gives all three modes at once.

**In Figma:** there is no tracing. Vectorise with one of the two above, then import the SVG (`handoff.md`).
