# Route B: raster first, then strokes

Natural, figurative people and animals are hard to build anchor by anchor: placed by hand from coordinates, a figure comes out stiff and simplified — a gingerbread figure, not an Entur figure — and an animal comes out geometric, without the observed detail of the library animals. For people, animals and scenes with them, generate a PNG first with an image model that takes the library's own illustrations as style references, review it, then turn it into strokes.

Use route A (the script) for signs, landscapes, and objects or vehicles made of exact geometry; it is exact and needs no credits. Everyday objects and vehicles in side view may come this way too, as the Figma plugin draws them.

## 1. Gather references

- **Style references** (image inputs): two to four standard-mode PNGs from the library that are closest to the motif, from `apps/documentation/src/downloads/illustrations/<Category>/<Motif>/PNG/`. For people pick people of the same kind and pose: Woman holding package, Man checking the time, Man and woman hugging, Woman walking with luggage; for staff add `Entur staff/Woman/PNG/Woman staff.png` or the man. For animals use animal PNGs. For objects: Ticket, Calendar, Clock, Notification, and also `Hands/Clapping`, `Hands/Thumbs up`, `Concepts/Data` and `Concepts/Digital Shared system`, the same object construction with Peach sparkle strokes, a Peach ring or Peach lines. For vehicles: the five in `Transport/`.
- **Newer people** (not in the downloads yet): the five newest sets in the People section of the Figma Illustration Library, made from the «People WIP» source files. They are Man standing with backpack, Woman conductor, Business traveller running (front and side view), Group trip with children, and Business traveller with suitcase. The Figma plugin keeps PNG exports of them as references. They carry more colour per figure than the older people: lavender clothes, Peach garments, several Peach items in the group, and coral staff panels on the conductor. Take line, proportion, clothing and pose from them, but keep the colour rules (rule 8): one Peach element, and coral only on Entur staff. Never mix in illustrations from outside the library.
- **Content reference:** the user's own photo, or a freely licensed one, may go in as an image input for pose and situation. A stock illustration or another illustrator's work (watermark, credit line) never goes in as an image input: the model copies what it is shown. Describe the scene in words instead and tell the user so in one line.

## 2. Generate

Use only tools Entur has approved: **Figma, Claude and Illustrator**. Figma's own image generation makes the draft and spends AI credits on the Figma plan; nothing else needs an account.

### Default: Figma image generation

- **In the Figma app** (a designer making the draft): Actions › Make an image, with two or three library PNGs attached through **+** as references and the prompt template below. References keep the line, the proportions and the colours closest to the library. Corrections go through Edit image on the draft (§3), which keeps what already works.
- **From Claude** (an agent making the draft): the Figma MCP tool `generate_image`, `model: "gemini-3.1-flash-image"`, `width` and `height` 2048, into a Figma file the user names or a new one (`create_new_file`). It takes a prompt only — no reference images, no draft to edit — so write the prompt as below under Fallback, and download the result from the `image.url` it returns. The gpt-image models it also offers are untested for this style.
- **Credits:** every run spends Figma AI credits on the file's plan, and each needs the user's approval, reruns included. So that this is one decision and not many, tell the user the whole plan before the first run — one generation plus up to two correction rounds — and then ask for each run as it comes.

### Fallback: prompt only

Without reference images (`generate_image`, or Make an image with nothing attached) the style drifts further; spell out every rule, including the line ("dark navy ink like a dip pen, swelling slightly in the middle and thinning at both ends") and the colours with their hex values. Drop the template's lines about attached references.

### Only where approved: Weave

Weave (app.weavy.ai, owned by Figma) runs Nano Banana 2.1 with reference images through the Figma MCP's `weave_*` tools, but it is a separate service with its own account, plan and credits, not part of the Figma licence. It is not approved at Entur: do not use it or offer it. Where an organisation has approved it, its runs ask for a cost approval each, like Figma's.

Ask for a square PNG, 2048 × 2048 where the model allows it. Use JPG only if the user asks for it: compression smears thin lines and makes tracing worse.

### Prompt template

Fill in the brackets; keep every rule, even the ones that seem obvious. A slot labelled [For …:] or [Optional for …:] belongs to the kinds it names (people, animals, objects, vehicles): for those, keep its text without the label; for the others, leave it out, and drop a bullet that is left empty. For an animal, the Peach element is never left out: name the prop or the marking (rule 19); for an object it is one small detail of it, never the whole object (rule 8). For coral take the first option — no coral or red — for animals, people, objects and scenes; the second only for a vehicle on its line, the third only for Entur staff (rule 8).

```text
Line illustration in exactly the style of the attached reference images (the Entur Linje illustration library).
Scene: [one sentence: who, doing what, with which one prop].

Style rules:
- Plain dark navy line (#181C56), even width, round line ends, a very slight hand-drawn tremor; some contours are left open.
- Flat fills only. No gradients, no shadows, no texture, no outlines thicker than the line.
- White (#FFFFFF) is the main fill. Lavender (#AEB7E2) for one garment. Peach (#FFBF9E) for exactly one element: [which]; it is the only highlight colour. [No coral or red anywhere, not on a comb, a wattle, a tongue, cheeks or clothes | Coral (#FF5959) only for the line under the vehicle | Coral (#FF5959) only for the Entur staff shoulder and sleeve panels].
- Colour fills sit slightly offset from their line, so a thin sliver of white shows along one side.
- [For people: people are drawn naturally, like an observed drawing: realistic adult proportions about 7.5 heads tall, a small head, a relaxed pose with weight on one leg or a slight turn, loose clothes with only 2–5 tapered fold marks, hands with fingers, shoes with soles.]
- [For people: faces are white and have NO EYES and NO MOUTH: only a nose line. Glasses are allowed.]
- [Entur staff: dark navy uniform with coral shoulder and sleeve panels and a small white name badge.]
- [For people: furniture is simplified: a table has four legs or none, and no leg crosses a person.]
- [Animals: drawn naturally, like an observed sketch of the real animal — correct anatomy, but minimal: long smooth contours with a few gaps, no fur ticks along the outline, no cluster of fur strokes on the chest or neck, at most three or four short fur marks in all and only where they show the species, one inner line per ear. The animal keeps a small dot eye and a small dark nose; no smile, no big cartoon eyes, no clothes. It has exactly one small peach (#FFBF9E) area, about 1 % of the image: a prop it holds, wears or has beside it (a collar, a ball, a ticket, a leaf), or one marking of its own (a chest patch, horns) as a flat shape with a rough edge and no outline. Peach on the body — a bird's beak or bill, comb, wattle and feet — is flat peach (#FFBF9E) with no dark outline around it; the head line stops where the beak begins but runs on under a comb; on a bird the beak and feet are its peach. The line swells slightly in the middle and thins at both ends, like a dip pen.]
- [For objects: one everyday thing on its own, slightly turned, no people or animals, no face; the line swells slightly like a dip pen; #EAEAF1 only on a side or back face.]
- [For vehicles: one vehicle in strict side view, long and low, facing right; white body; windows as solid navy fills with one or two short white glint strokes; no people visible; no disc; under it one coral (#FF5959) line: solid for rails, dashed for a road, a gentle wave for water.]
- Plain white background. [For animals and people: one simple thin navy ground line under the subject, in one or two long pieces, running a little past it: no row of dashes, no second line, no grass unless the scene asks for it — and then only one small tuft of two or three blades at the foot of something.] [For objects: no ground line: the object floats, alone or on the disc.] [Optional for animals: a soft pale grey-lavender disc (#EAEAF1) behind the upper body.] [Optional for objects: a soft pale grey-lavender disc (#EAEAF1) behind the object.]
- No text, no logos, no frame. Square, 1:1.

Use the references only for line, proportion and colour. Do not copy any figure or composition from them.
```

## 3. Review the PNG — before the user sees it

Review every draft yourself; never show a first draft as a result or wait for the user to point out what this review catches. Look at it next to the style references at the same size and run the Step 5 checklist in `SKILL.md`. Write down every deviation, fix them in an edit round, and review again. Up to two edit rounds; then trace. Models tend to slip on the same things:

- eyes or a mouth drawn anyway, or a cartoon face
- accessories drawn twice (two earcups, a split headband)
- big heads, blocky bodies, mitten hands — the corporate-illustration habit
- gradients, soft shadows, extra colours, a coloured background
- lines too thick or too even, like a vector icon
- text or logos
- the ground as a row of dashes, as two lines or heavier than the contours; grass tufts along it or on both sides when the scene does not need nature
- animals: geometric or blobby shapes, a cartoon face, big eyes or a smile, soft grey gradients in the fur, the subject small in the frame (library animals fill about 60–75 % of the height)
- animals: more line than the library animal next to it — fur ticks along the back, a cluster of fur strokes on the chest or neck, several lines inside an ear (rule 18)
- animals: no Peach at all, or Peach spread over the body instead of one prop or marking (rule 19)
- peach on the body outlined in navy — a beak, a comb, a wattle, feet — or a beak in another colour: ask for "the beak, comb, wattle and feet are flat peach shapes with no outline; the head line stops at the beak and runs on under the comb" (rule 20)
- coral or red on an animal, a person or an object — a hen's comb and wattle, a tongue, cheeks, a garment: make it Peach or paper in an edit round (rule 8)
- a sign, prop or tail closer than 16 units to the canvas edge

Do not repair pixels, and do not start over. **In the Figma app**, select the draft, choose Edit image, attach the library reference that shows the fix, and ask for only the changes with the prompt below. **Through `generate_image`**, which has no image input, run the full prompt again with the same numbered changes appended as "Corrections from the last attempt", the pose and what worked described in words, and keep whichever draft is closer:

```text
Edit the first image. Keep everything else exactly as it is: [pose, hands, face, props, line, colours].
Make only these changes:
1. [UNIFORM: coral ONLY as a shoulder yoke and one narrow sleeve stripe, like the second image.]
2. [...]
The face keeps NO EYES and NO MOUTH, only the nose. No text. Square 1:1.
```

An edit keeps what already works; a new generation throws it away, which is why Edit image in the app is the better correction when a designer is at hand. One or two correction rounds are normal, and each costs the same as a generation. Near-white noise in the background (values around 249–255) is invisible and does not survive tracing; it needs no cleaning.

The approved PNG is a **draft**: fine for a sketch, a concept or a review, never as the final library asset.

## 4. Turn it into strokes

Final assets are live strokes in three colour modes.

**In Illustrator, automated** (macOS, Illustrator running; no plugin):

```bash
node scripts/illustrator-trace.mjs <draft.png> --out <dir> --name <Name>
```

For an animal or object add `--construction animals --carriers <profiles.ai> --backdrop --underlay` (details below), and for an object `--object` as well (below).

It drives Illustrator through AppleScript `do javascript` and writes `<Name>.ai` with live strokes, `<Name>.svg`, `<Name>-darkmode.svg` and `<Name>-contrast.svg`. Options: `--app` for another Illustrator install (default Illustrator 2026), `--size` (artboard in pt, default 360, which puts a figure at library scale), `--threshold` (line darkness, default 110), `--max-stroke` (widest line traced as a stroke, default 20; at 14 the thicker pieces of a contour drop out), `--contour-share` (share of lines that get the contour weight, default 0.25), `--roughen` (add Roughen to the strokes; off by default), `--fill-rough` and `--ink-shift` (rough fill edges and offset ink clothing; off, and not in the reference drawings), `--no-features` (leave out the eyes and noses put back on animals, below), `--object` (an object: no eyes or noses, and the disc's pale colour only on its faces, below), `--vehicle` (a vehicle's body and window roles, below), `--no-simplify` (keep every traced line on an animal, below). A 2048 px draft can take several minutes, more with other large documents open. The same work, and the finishing steps below, are available to agents as MCP tools (`illustrator_status`, `illustrator_run_jsx`, `illustrator_trace_draft`, `illustrator_scale_head`, `illustrator_smooth_region`, `illustrator_add_details`):

```bash
claude mcp add -s local illustrator-bridge -- node <skill folder>/scripts/illustrator-mcp.mjs
```

What the script does, and why — the same steps apply when you trace by hand:

- **Line layer:** Black and White trace, **Strokes only**, method **Abutting**. With Fills on, the lines merge with the ink areas they touch (uniform, hair) into one filled region. With Overlapping, white areas are shapes stacked on a solid silhouette, and Ignore White deletes them, leaving a filled silhouette.
- **Fill layer:** Limited colours, Abutting, Fills. Every area snaps to the nearest palette colour. Enclosed white is kept as `paper`, because it turns light grey in dark mode; only the white background that touches the artboard edge is removed. Ink areas stay only when they are wide (mean width over ~4 pt: a uniform, hair, shoes); thin line-like ink is dropped, since the line layer draws it. On animals the ink comes from the draft's pixels instead (below, Ink masses).
- **Strokes:** ink colour, round caps and joins. The thickest quarter of the lines gets 1.5 pt, the rest 1.0 pt — by traced width, or by length (long outer contours) when the draft's lines are all equally thick. Library people land at about that mix; an all-1.5 figure looks heavy and coarse next to them.
- **Fills smooth, lightly shifted.** A low path fidelity keeps colour areas to a few anchors instead of a jagged edge, and no Roughen goes on fills. Colour areas (not ink) are shifted 0.6 pt right and 0.3 pt down, the size of the offsets in hand-made reference people.
- **No Roughen by default.** The draft already carries the hand tremor; Roughen on top makes lines bumpy, and on large fills a relative Roughen makes edges hairy. Use `--roughen` only when the traced lines look too clean.
- **Modes by palette mapping.** Every white becomes `paper`; recolour white clothing to `cloth` by hand in the dark file if the motif has it. With `--vehicle` the script gives the vehicle its roles itself (below). The backdrop circle shares its standard colour with `tint`, but the script gives the Circle layer the `backdrop` values from `palette.md` in the dark and contrast files: Ebony 20 at 30 % opacity and Blue 90, where small tint fills turn Ebony 30 and Blue 40.

With `--construction animals`, for animals and objects:

- **Artboard and weights:** 320 pt, contour 2.1 and detail 1.7 at the widest point.
- **Width profiles** (`--carriers`): every traced line gets a live width profile, copied from the strokes named `swell` and `point` in the carriers file (`handoff.md`); lines shorter than 12 pt, such as fur marks, get `point`.
- **Backdrop** (`--backdrop`): the traced disc is lumpy and often traces as a white interior inside a grey rim. It is replaced by the library's 4-anchor circle with Roughen 0.65 % on a Circle layer, and the traced white interior and rim fragments are removed. The circle is measured in the draft's pixels — the largest connected area of the disc's pale grey-lavender, its radius from the longer side, measured from the top — so it still comes in when the subject covers a third of the disc or more (Hens family), and a grey wing beside it does not stretch it. Only a plausible disc counts — at least 30 % of the width, round, at least a third filled, near the middle — so a draft without one (two seagulls in flight, with pale wings) gets no circle: the library has animals with and without the disc.
- **Underlay** (`--underlay`): paper under the animal, traced from its silhouette in the draft. Without it, white areas the colour trace cannot tell from the background — a tail that reaches past the disc — stay unfilled, and the ground line shows through. Every figure gets its own shape (a chick standing apart from the hen too), holes in the silhouette stay open, the paper stops at the top of the ground line except inside a closed figure that crosses it (an egg sitting in the grass), a body the circle cuts is continued past it (below), and pockets closed off between grass blades and the line are left out; each would show as a pale patch in dark mode. The paper comes in a second pass, after the lines exist: the draft's silhouette is cut back to the finished lines (gaps up to 2 % of the width closed), so its edge sits under the middle of every line — not past a line end that thins, and not past a fur mark the simplify step removed, which left a white rim in dark mode. White and grey slivers the colour trace keeps in the draft's thin gaps between a line and its fill (mean width under 1.5 pt; grey only under 20 pt²) go, and so does every white piece lying mostly outside the figures, judged by points over its area (a white triangle between a disc's edge, a sign and its post) — outside both the draft's silhouette and the closed finished lines, which leaves an open cloud out even where it closes against a wing in the draft. Those showed as a white double line along the seagulls' cloud and white strips outside their contours in dark mode. Large pieces of the traced disc cut off by a line (under the doves' wire) go with the backdrop step: a lighter patch in dark mode. Inside the disc white counts as figure, but outside it a fluffy outline's gaps let the background in, so the draft's paper can stop exactly at the circle's edge (a white Samoyed with no paper below the circle). Where the paper is cut along a good stretch of the rim (5 % of the width or more), it is continued into what the finished lines enclose (gaps up to 1.6 % of the width closed), through the gaps of the outline but never across a drawn line, down to the ground line; a white pocket that only touches the rim for a few pixels (between a hen and an egg) stays open. Then the paper's edge is fitted to the strokes (rule 21): pass one writes the strokes' geometry, and walking round each piece of paper every contour stroke hands over to the next with a straight chord — from its free end on in its own direction to the next stroke, else from end to end — and paper past the chords goes while a dent short of them fills. Nothing changes deeper than 1.5 % of the width, no chord runs to the ground line (the gap between two feet stays background), and the ground band keeps its footprint. On the Samoyed this took the visible difference from a designer's hand-cut paper down by about 40 %. The final mask is smoothed (a 4 px blur at 1024, halved), so a ragged edge does not trace as stairs.
- **Ink masses:** dark wing and tail tips, a nose or a dark patch, with white streaks between the feathers. The mean-width rule drops those (four of six seagull tips went), so on animals the ink is opened in the draft instead — eroded by about 4 px at 1024, grown back and cut with the ink again, so the gaps stay white — and every piece of at least 400 px at 1024 is traced as an ink fill on top of the Fill layer, in a group named Ink. Lines are too thin to survive the opening; pieces lying wholly in the ground band (a thick dash) are left to the ground step. In Figma they bind to the same line variable as the outline, as on the library doves.
- **Vehicles** (`--vehicle`, people construction): the dark and contrast files give the library's vehicle roles. White areas become the `vehicle` body (Ebony 40 in dark mode, white in contrast). The windows' ink fills become `window` (Canary 30 in dark mode, Blue 70 in contrast), and the paper glints inside them stay paper. A window is a solid dark area of the draft once its lines are opened away: at least 1.5 % of the width across, filling most of its box, and centred in the upper three quarters of the vehicle. Wheels and small dark details sit lower or are smaller. On the library's bus, train, tram, boat and scooter this finds exactly the fills their dark files light up (5, 4, 4, 8 and none). The colour trace loses the body: its white joins the background through the open contour. So `--vehicle` brings the paper underlay as the body, with gaps up to 2 % of the width closed, walls at the left and right edge of the ink, and gaps along a row of up to 6 % of the width bridged: a bus's skirt line stops short of each wheel. A train's last car, which runs off with its lines and no end line, stays part of the body, as in the library's train. The window fills stay in the lines the underlay is cut back to, so a windscreen closes the nose.
- **Objects** (`--object`): an object gets no eyes or noses, and inside the circle the disc's pale grey-lavender stays only on a face of the object: a pale area closed in by lines that lies on the object (paper makes up 40 % or more of the ring just beyond its line), such as a card's chip. Everything else pale there is the disc showing through, and the colour trace kept it as a tint fill: an opaque grey in dark mode and a light rim in contrast mode. A strip between a line and its paper, where the draft's paper sits a little off the line, goes, and the paper under it shows. The inside of an empty ring on the disc gets neither tint nor paper, so the circle shows there in every mode, as in the library. Found on the rebate ticket: a grey dot in an empty ring in dark mode and light rims at the notches in contrast mode.
- **Eyes and noses** (on by default): a small dark eye or nose is too thin for the colour trace and too round for the line trace, so it drops out of both. The script finds them in the draft — dots that stand free, and thick spots left once the lines are eroded away, such as a nose at the end of the snout line — and puts each back as an ink ellipse on a Features layer on top. People never get them: an Entur person has no eyes (rule 13), and glasses are lines the line trace keeps. Spots in the band just above the ground line are never taken: there they are grass clumps, not eyes (Hens family had nine). A smaller animal in a group has a smaller eye, so a dot down to about 21 px in a 1024 px draft still counts when it is round, solid and stands free of other ink (the second seagull's eye).
- **Simplify** (on by default; `--no-simplify` turns it off): the draft usually has more line than a library animal (rule 18). Before the weights go on, the script drops specks under 3 pt, joins a contour broken into pieces end to end (within 4 pt, running on within 45°), replaces the ground with one plain line, and thins short fur marks (under 20 pt): a mark lying along a contour goes, and of marks within 20 pt of each other only the longest stays. Marks within 20 pt of an eye or nose (a mouth, an inner ear line), toes and grass stay. On the bear cub it took the trace from 80 to about 50 strokes. The ground: where the draft has a ground line (one dark run across a quarter of the width, found in its pixels), the traced dashes and short grass marks in that band go, and one plain line takes their place — ink, detail weight, Roughen 0.2 %, from the first to the last piece of the drawn line — on a Ground layer under the fills, so the figures hide it where they stand. A short mark there that touches a leg is a foot and stays. Without a ground line in the draft, the dashes are joined into pieces at detail weight instead.

**Check the trace against the draft.** The trace must reproduce the draft — nothing lost, nothing added — except what the skill changes on purpose. After every trace, and before the finishing steps:

```bash
node scripts/trace-check.mjs <draft.png> <Name>.svg [--out <dir>] [--json] [--object] [--vehicle]
```

It renders the standard SVG at the draft's size (macOS `qlmanage` and `sips`, no dependencies, under a second at 1024 px) and compares pixel classes: ink by luminance, peach, coral, lavender, tint and paper by the nearest palette colour. It reports, in artboard units, ink missing from the trace (typed dot, mass or line: a lost eye, a dark wing tip, a line), ink added, colour areas lost or added, white fill outside the figures (a crescent or strip that only shows in dark mode), paper along the outside of a line (a white rim or double line, also only in dark mode), and paper missing inside a figure (the dark surface inside an outline — white in the draft, closed in both the draft and the trace; an opening between legs that closes against the ground line is left out). A piece cut from the edge of a larger mark is ignored unless it is large, so contour rounding is not noise. It writes `trace-check.json` and `trace-check.png`, the draft faded with every finding boxed and numbered: red missing, blue extra, magenta paper outside.

Read every finding. **Intended:** fur and feather marks the simplify step thinned, grass and dashes the plain ground line replaced, tint differences at the rim of the redrawn library circle, a red part turned Peach. For an object traced with `--object`, pass `--object` to the check too: pale strips that became paper on purpose are then not reported. For a vehicle pass `--vehicle`: its open ends are closed as in the trace, so a train's last car is not reported as paper outside the figure. **A fault:** anything else — fix it in the trace or by hand, and say in the result what is left. Two faults it found and the trace now handles: a transparent draft traced as one dark square (drafts with an alpha channel are flattened on white first), and birds on a wire whose wire was taken for ground (a long line with tails and feet below it is a perch and stays as drawn).

When `illustrator-trace.mjs` changes, run the trace and the check on a regression set before committing: the drafts behind Hens family, the two seagulls, the bear cub and the fox, and the library's `Animals/Doves/PNG/Doves-background.png`. Known gaps: the doves' tiny eyes are still missed, and a part whose outline has a gap wider than about 10 px at 1024 in the draft (the left seagull's far wing) gets no paper — trace-check reports it as paper missing inside a figure; add the paper by hand.

**In Illustrator, by hand:**

1. Place the PNG on a 360 pt artboard and lock it on a layer at 50 % opacity.
2. Line layer: Object > Image Trace, Mode Black and White, threshold so only the navy line survives, method Abutting. Under Advanced, create **Strokes** only, with a stroke width limit around 20 px for a 2048 px draft. Expand.
3. Select all lines. Stroke panel: 1.0 pt, Cap Round, Corner Round, Profile Uniform; then select the long outer contours and set them to 1.5 pt (about a quarter of the lines). Join broken pieces, delete specks; aim for few anchors.
4. Fill layer: trace the PNG again with Mode Limited colours, method Abutting, a low path fidelity and the palette as the swatch set, create Fills, delete the background white, nudge one to three colour areas 0.6 pt right and 0.3 pt down, and put the layer under the lines.
5. Build the dark and contrast versions with Recolor Artwork and the mapping in `palette.md`. Add the Roughen graphic style from `handoff.md` only if the lines look too clean.

**Finish** — the trace gives the construction; these steps give it the reference drawings' simplicity, proportions and life. Points are Illustrator points, y up; read them off a grid render of the traced file.

1. **Simplify accessories:** a traced headset, pair of glasses or strap often comes out doubled — a cushion plus a ring, a band split in two by a line. Delete the extra parts and redraw each as one shape (one band stroke, one earcup). Where a removed part cut through a silhouette, the silhouette keeps a slot; close it by removing those anchors (`illustrator_run_jsx`).
2. **Head size:** compare with a library person at the same height. If the head is bigger, run `illustrator_scale_head` with the neck point, the scale (0.8–0.9) and the head column. It scales head parts as wholes and moves the head anchors of any silhouette that runs into the body with a soft blend, so hair inside a hair-and-uniform shape shrinks too. Scaling parts one by one misses that hair, and the head keeps looking big.
3. **Smooth the hair:** `illustrator_smooth_region` over the hair (default: every fourth anchor kept, three passes) gives one smooth edge. It also flattens small bumps inside the region, such as a microphone tip; redraw those afterwards.
4. **Fold and sheen marks:** add 2–5 tapered marks with the MCP tool `illustrator_add_details` (points in Illustrator points, y up; read them off a grid render of the traced file), or by hand with a width profile or a small filled shape. Paper-white on ink or colour, ink on light fills.
5. **Simplify props:** remove a lone table leg, or any leg that crosses a person; four legs or none.
6. **Animals — eyes and noses:** the trace puts them back on the Features layer. Check that each is there and the right size; draw one it missed as a small ink ellipse, and delete one that is not a feature (a dark button or hoof the colour trace already kept).
7. **Ground:** one simple line at detail weight, in one to three pieces (`style.md` §4), always behind the figure. On animals the trace replaces it with one plain line on a Ground layer under the fills, and the body's paper covers the line's thickness where the body stands on it (paws, a haunch, a tail on the ground) — not below it, so no pale strip shows under the feet. On people move the ground line to a layer under the fills by hand where it crosses the figure (a coat hem, a chair base), so their fills hide it — the trace leaves it on top, because the colour trace's thin white strips around the dashes would cover it there; join the dashes and set the weight by hand. Grass is not removed automatically (a toe and a blade look alike to the trace): keep at most one tuft of two or three blades, where the scene gives it a reason (a pole, a bench leg), and delete the rest.
8. **Animals — less line:** look at the result next to a library animal at the same size. Delete what the simplify step left that the library would not draw: a fur tick on the back, a second mark on the chest, a staircase of short pieces along a contour (join those into one stroke). One fold line at the neck or shoulder can replace a run of fur.
9. **Animals — Peach:** the trace keeps a Peach area from the draft and shifts it off its line. If the draft brought none, add one now on the Fill layer, about 1 % of the canvas: a prop gets its own ink outline with the Peach shifted 0.6 pt off it; a marking on the body (a chest patch) has no outline and Roughen 1.5 %, relative, detail 51.
10. **Animals — peach on the body:** if the trace still has ink lines around a beak, comb, wattle, feet or another peach body part, delete the strokes that belong to the part — every anchor along its outer edge, away from the body, such as a comb's scalloped top. Keep the head's or body's own contour where it runs along the part's base (the forehead line under a comb), even when all its anchors lie within 1.6 pt of the peach: closeness alone also catches that line. A stroke that runs on toward another feature (the beak) is the head's, not the part's. Cut each contour that only passes the peach so it ends about 2 pt from it — at the boundary anchor, splitting the curve, not by dropping anchors; at a beak the head line stops with that gap. Then grow the peach shape by about 0.9 pt (Offset Path, expanded) so it covers where the line ran, a beak's tip a little past the old tip (rule 20). Horns and props keep their lines. Copying a path into another open document with `duplicate()` fails (MRAP): copy its anchors and handles instead.
11. **Animals — the paper's edge (rule 21):** the trace fits the paper to the strokes (Underlay above); check it in the dark-mode file. Two places it leaves for you: paper behind a fur tuft that curls outward off the contour, where a hand-cut edge runs straight past it from the stroke before to the stroke after, and paper between two feet on the ground line, where the line should show. Edit the Underlay shape there (Direct Selection, or Shape Builder against the strokes). trace-check reports paper along the outside of a line; a dent between strokes it does not report.

**In code** (no Illustrator): `node scripts/grid.mjs <draft.png> --format square`, render the grid, and redraw the centre lines with the script (route A), 12–25 anchors per contour of a person. Slower, and it loses some of the PNG's life, but it stays in the repository and gives all three modes at once.

**In Figma:** there is no tracing. Vectorise with one of the two above, then import the SVG (`handoff.md`).
